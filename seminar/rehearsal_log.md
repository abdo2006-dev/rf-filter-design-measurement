## Rehearsal rules

1. **Out loud, standing, timed.** Reading silently takes about half as long as speaking and tells
   you nothing.
2. **Record the first one.** Watching it is unpleasant and is the fastest improvement available.
3. **Time each act separately**, not just the total — the total hides which act is the problem.
4. **Rehearse at least once in front of a person who is not an engineer.** Ideally a maths
   student, i.e. the actual audience. Ask them afterwards what the talk was about; if they cannot
   say it in one sentence, Act I needs work.
5. **Do not fix overrun by talking faster.** Cut slides. See the cut order in
   `SEMINAR_MASTER_PLAN.md`.

## Cut order

When over time, cut in this order:

1. Act VI detail (simulation workflow — engineers care, mathematicians less so)
2. Act V depth (filter theory beyond what Act VIII needs)
3. Act II detail (wave mechanics beyond Γ)
4. Act III detail (reduce the four S-parameters to "reflection and transmission, both ways")

**Never cut Acts IV or VIII.** They are the talk. A 30-minute emergency version is Acts I, IV,
VIII and IX, and it still works as a complete talk.
